Why liquid hydrogen boils away

A layer-resolved model of multilayer insulation, coupled to a two-phase boil-off model and a cost module.

Under review

The problem

Liquid hydrogen sits at about 20 K. Heat leaks in through the insulation, a fraction of the liquid evaporates, and that gas has to be vented, reliquefied or used. The size of the loss sets much of the cost of storage.

What I built

Most boil-off models treat the insulation as a single block with one effective conductivity. I built a model in Python that resolves every layer of the multilayer insulation at every timestep. It is coupled to a two-phase thermodynamic model of the tank and to an economic module.

Results

A global sensitivity analysis over seven design variables puts vacuum pressure first, with an index of 12.76. Fill level comes second at 9.60, then insulation layer count at 5.80.

Over a 90-day run the boil-off fraction rose from under 10% to over 50% as the vacuum degraded from 5×10⁻⁴ Pa to 0.46 Pa. The storage-related levelised cost of hydrogen came to between $6.19 and $6.88 per kilogram. The best fill level is about 75%, and the best insulation layer count sits between 60 and 70.
