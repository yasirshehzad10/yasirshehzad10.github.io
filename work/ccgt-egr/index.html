Cutting the energy cost of capture at a gas power plant

Recirculating part of a gas turbine's exhaust makes the CO₂ easier to capture.

34.5%less reboiler heat in the capture unit, at an exhaust recirculation ratio of 0.33

The problem

Capturing CO₂ from a gas power plant costs energy. Regenerating the solvent takes steam from the plant, so net output drops. The exhaust is also dilute, at 5.44% CO₂, so the capture unit treats a lot of gas for a little carbon.

What I built

I built an Aspen Plus model of a full combined cycle plant: gas turbine, heat recovery steam generator, steam turbine and an MEA absorption unit. Then I sent part of the exhaust back to the turbine inlet and swept the recirculation ratio.

Results

At a ratio of 0.33, flue gas CO₂ rose from 5.44% to 8.22% and oxygen fell from 10.56% to 5.61%. Reboiler heat fell by 34.5% and net power output dropped by less than 0.5%. The steam cost of capture fell from $14.23 to $9.26 per tonne of CO₂.

Of that 34.5% drop, 33 points come from the smaller exhaust flow reaching the capture unit. The rest comes from a lower liquid-to-gas ratio.

What limits it

Going past a ratio of 0.33 does not work in practice. Gas turbine combustors need at least 16% oxygen at the inlet, and a higher ratio would need changes to the turbine.
