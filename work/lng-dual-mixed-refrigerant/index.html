Less power to liquefy natural gas

A dual mixed refrigerant cycle, with the composition of both refrigerants tuned together.

26%less compressor power, from 530.5 to 392.4 kJ per kg of LNG

The problem

Liquefying natural gas is mostly a compressor problem. Most of the energy goes into the refrigeration loops that cool the gas, so any saving there shows up straight in the operating bill.

A dual mixed refrigerant process uses two loops, and each loop carries a blend of several components. The blend decides how hard the compressors work. Twelve coupled composition variables are too many to tune by hand.

What I built

The cycle makes LNG at 27 bar. That lets the product leave at about −112 °C instead of −160 °C, which trims the refrigeration load before any optimisation starts.

I modelled the cycle in Aspen Plus and cross-checked it in Aspen HYSYS. Then I wrote a genetic algorithm in MATLAB that talks to Aspen Plus through COM. It searches the composition of both refrigerants at once, and the simulator scores every candidate.

Results

Compressor power fell 26%, from 530.5 to 392.4 kJ per kg of LNG, against the baseline cycle the study started from. Intercooler duty dropped by about 13%. Counting utilities only, operating cost fell 23%, from $14.61 to $11.23 per tonne of LNG.

The search converged in 10 generations, after testing 5,000 combinations of the two refrigerant compositions. A sensitivity analysis showed that a small subset of the composition variables drove most of the objective.

What limits it

This is a steady-state simulation. Exchangers have no pressure drop, heat losses from pipes and vessels are ignored, and refrigerant flow rates, temperatures and pressures stay at the baseline design values, so only the compositions move.
